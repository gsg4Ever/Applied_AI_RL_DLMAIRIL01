## 2. Detailed explanation in my own words

### Learn values, or learn the policy directly?

A value-based method learns $V(s)$ or $Q(s,a)$ and obtains a policy indirectly:

$$
\pi(s)\in\operatorname*{argmax}_aQ(s,a).
$$

The Q-value evaluates the discounted continuation associated with the action, not merely its immediate reward. Enumerating actions becomes difficult when there are very many or continuously varying choices.

A policy-based method directly parameterizes the action-selection rule, $\pi_\theta$. A deterministic policy chooses one action for a given state. A stochastic policy assigns probabilities to actions given that state, written here as $\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$.

### Optimize total future reward

The goal is the expected discounted return under the chosen policy:

$$
J(\theta)=
\mathbb E_{\pi_\theta}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^kr_{t+k}\right].
$$

The return from one trajectory, denoted $v_t$ in the book, is

$$
v_t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^kr_{t+k}.
$$

For finite episodes, this sum ends at termination; discounting can be retained with $\gamma=1$ for the undiscounted case. The expectation can be estimated by averaging trajectory returns generated with the current policy.

The source also expresses the objective using a policy-induced distribution of states and their values. The central point is that changing the policy changes the trajectories and therefore their expected return.

### Gradient ascent

Choose policy parameters that maximize $J$. Iteratively update

$$
\theta'\leftarrow\theta+\alpha\nabla_\theta J(\theta).
$$

The plus sign denotes **ascent**, because return is maximized. The policy-gradient relation presented by the source is

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)=
\mathbb E_{\pi_\theta}
\left[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\,
Q^{\pi_\theta}(s,a)\right].
$$

The expectation is approximated with interactions sampled under the current policy. Action-value information weights the change in action-selection preference.

The presented method is **on-policy**: samples are generated by the policy being optimized. The source's basic procedure obtains new samples after changing the policy rather than treating old-policy data as unchanged current-policy data.

### REINFORCE

Use the actual sampled future return $v_i$ as an estimate of the action value:

$$
\theta\leftarrow\theta+
\alpha\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_i\mid s_i)v_i.
$$

The source algorithm initializes parameters, generates a trajectory under the current policy, computes returns for its steps, updates parameters with those returns, and samples again using the updated policy.

Value learning and policy learning therefore optimize behavior through different intermediate objects: one learns values and selects their best actions; the other learns the parameters of action selection itself.

## 3. Key terms

| Term | Meaning in this section |
|---|---|
| Implicit policy | An action rule derived from learned values. |
| Stochastic policy | Action probabilities conditioned on the current state. |
| Policy parameters | Learned quantities controlling action selection. |
| Objective J | Expected return to maximize. |
| Gradient ascent | Update parameters in a direction increasing the objective. |
| REINFORCE | A policy-gradient update using sampled future returns. |

## 4. Important formulas / algorithms / steps

$$
J(\theta)=\mathbb E_{\pi_\theta}\left[\sum_k\gamma^kr_{t+k}\right],
\qquad \theta'\leftarrow\theta+\alpha\nabla_\theta J(\theta).
$$

REINFORCE:

$$
\theta\leftarrow\theta+
\alpha\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_i\mid s_i)v_i.
$$

Value-based: derive actions from Q. Policy-based: optimize action-selection parameters directly.

## 5. Source-book example

The source REINFORCE procedure samples states, actions and rewards under one policy, then uses the future discounted reward from each visited step to weight the policy update. The same sampled trajectory supplies several step-specific returns, rather than one fixed label for every action.

## 8. Active recall

Answer before expanding the source-derived answer.

> [!question]- 1. How does value-based learning obtain a policy?
> Choose actions with the highest learned Q-values.

> [!question]- 2. What does policy-based learning optimize directly?
> Parameters of the action-selection policy.

> [!question]- 3. How do deterministic and stochastic policies differ?
> One chooses a fixed action for a state; the other assigns action probabilities.

> [!question]- 4. What does the policy objective J represent?
> Expected cumulative discounted reward under the policy.

> [!question]- 5. Why does the parameter update use gradient ascent?
> The objective is to maximize expected return.

> [!question]- 6. What weights the source policy-gradient expression?
> The action's value under the current policy.

> [!question]- 7. What does REINFORCE substitute for that action value?
> The observed future discounted return from the trajectory.

> [!question]- 8. Why is the presented policy-gradient approach on-policy?
> Its samples come from the policy currently being evaluated and improved.
