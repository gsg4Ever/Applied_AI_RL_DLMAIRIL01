Core explanation

Bandit decisions must earn reward and gather information simultaneously; regret measures the reward sacrificed relative to the best-known-mean choice, and insufficient continued sampling can lock a policy into an inferior source.

Key terms

TermMeaning
Bandit armA reward source producing a random outcome when selected.
Reward meanExpected reward from one source.
Fixed reward alternativeA source with a known constant payoff.
Action frequencyNumber of observations obtained from a particular arm.
Bandit policyRule selecting an arm from available history or its summaries.
RegretBest achievable expected reward minus the policy's expected reward.
Frozen rankingA choice based on estimates that receive no further corrective samples.

A selected arm updates its count and mean; unselected arms do not gain observations. A fixed initial sample followed by permanent commitment can preserve a wrong ranking indefinitely.

Source-book example

In the source's one-bandit-versus-fixed-reward example, permanently accepting the fixed reward after an underestimated initial bandit sample prevents any correction. Continuing to update only the selected source solves overestimation of the bandit, but not underestimation that stops its sampling.

Active recall

Answer before expanding. Open the answers before printing to include them.

What is a one-armed bandit in this book?

A source that produces a random reward with an unknown mean when selected.

If the bandit mean is known, when should it beat the fixed alternative?

When its mean reward exceeds the fixed reward.

What does a stage discount sequence define?

How successive rewards are weighted in the accumulated objective.

What is the incremental reward-estimate update?

Q becomes Q plus learning rate times observed reward minus Q.

Which count determines a running mean's step size?

The number of observations from that particular reward source.

What is regret?

The best achievable expected total reward minus the policy's expected total reward.

Why can fixed initial sampling followed by permanent commitment fail?

A finite-sample ranking error can persist because the rejected source is never sampled again.

What remains wrong with updating only the chosen bandit?

An initially underestimated bandit that is no longer chosen still receives no corrective data.
