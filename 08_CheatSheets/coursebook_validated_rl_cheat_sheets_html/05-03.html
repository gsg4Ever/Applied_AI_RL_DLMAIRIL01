Core explanation

Exploration trades current reward against useful information: epsilon-greedy and Boltzmann randomize actions, posterior/bootstrapped methods represent uncertainty, curiosity rewards controllable learning, and model-based methods use known-state tests, optimism or prioritized updates.

Active recall

Answer before expanding. Open the answers before printing to include them.

What can greedy exploitation miss?

Better actions that current experience has not revealed.

How does Boltzmann exploration differ from uniform random exploration?

Its action probabilities depend on relative Q estimates.

What happens to finite-action Boltzmann probabilities at high temperature?

They approach a uniform distribution.

What information guides Thompson sampling?

Bayesian posterior information updated from observations.

How does bootstrapped DQN represent uncertainty?

Maintain multiple Q estimates and use a randomly selected estimate for behavior.

Why add intrinsic curiosity reward?

External rewards may be too sparse to guide exploration.

Why not reward prediction errors from rustling leaves?

Those changes are outside the agent's control and distract from useful learning.

When does an E3 state become known?

After enough observations to estimate rewards and transitions sufficiently accurately.

How does E3 act in unknown states?

Try a least-sampled action there.

What reward does R-max assign to unknown pairs?

A fixed optimistic R-max proxy until enough observations replace it.

Does Dyna alone define a behavior action policy?

No; action selection is a separate choice.

What discrepancy motivates prioritized sweeping?

The absolute TD error above a chosen threshold.
